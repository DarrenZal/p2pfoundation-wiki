# Interview Guides — P2P Foundation Wiki Evolution

**Purpose:** Stewardship-first discovery before committing to a research brief or platform direction. Three audiences: Michel (editorial steward), Jeff (infrastructure steward), active contributor. Run before (or alongside) Report A so findings are grounded in lived practice.

**Status:** DRAFT — refine with Darren + collaborators before running.

---

## Michel Bauwens — editorial steward (~45 min)

1. Walk me through how you add or update a wiki page today. What's open on your screen, what steps do you take, what tools do you use?
2. What's the piece of the workflow that most makes you stop or postpone writing? What would unblock you?
3. When you look at the wiki as it stands now, which parts feel most alive to you, and which parts are most in need of care?
4. The `Category:Housing` AI-summary experiment you showed — what were you hoping for, and what fell short?
5. If an AI assistant could take one kind of wiki work off your hands, what would it be? And what's the one kind you would never want it touching?
6. How do you feel about co-editors and contributors working alongside you on the wiki? Where's the line for you between helpful collaboration and dilution of the editorial vision?
7. What languages does this wiki most need but doesn't have, and who would do that work?
8. What about your way of curating this wiki would you most want the next generation of stewards to learn from — the part that isn't already written down anywhere?

---

## Jeff Emmett — infrastructure steward (~30 min)

1. What does running wiki.p2pfoundation.net actually cost you in time and money per month, and what causes the peaks?
2. What's the top source of operational pain — spam, broken extensions, backups, version drift, uptime, something else?
3. How do you feel about structured-data extensions on the live wiki (Semantic MediaWiki, Wikibase, NeoWiki, anything else)? Where's your appetite, where's your concern?
4. If the wiki were to host a public SPARQL/REST API and bulk dumps for external researchers, what would you need from collaborators to make that sustainable — technically and over time?
5. Who else has admin access today, and what does the human bus factor look like if you had to step back for a month?

---

## Active contributor (~30 min)

1. How did you first find your way to contributing to this wiki? What drew you, what made you stay, what made contributing hard?
2. Walk me through the most recent time you tried to contribute. What worked, what blocked you?
3. Which other wikis or knowledge commons do you contribute to, and what's the experience like there compared to here?
4. If you could change one thing about the contributor experience on wiki.p2pfoundation.net, what would it be?
5. What would get you to contribute more? What would make you stop?

---

## Notes for the interviewer

- Let silences sit. Long-tenured stewards often answer the second question you asked, not the first.
- Follow the emotional energy. If Michel lights up about one topic, abandon the script for 10 minutes.
- Record with consent. Transcribe via Otter or MacWhisper. Re-read before the next interview — patterns appear across conversations.
- Ask for artifacts: screenshots of their actual editing session, links to pages they're proud of, links to ones they've given up on. Concrete beats abstract.
- At the end of each interview, ask: *"Who else should I talk to who'd disagree with you?"* That single question usually triples the contact list.
